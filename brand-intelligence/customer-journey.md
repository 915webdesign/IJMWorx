## 1. Discovery

**How they find IJM:**
- **Google Search** — "pool builders el paso," "pergola builder el paso," "outdoor kitchen el paso," "landscaping el paso," and dozens of service + city variations. The primary paid and organic discovery channel. *(Today, paid search covers pool terms only — every other service is invisible in ads.)*
- **Google Maps / GBP (the Map Pack)** — the local 3-pack. Reviews and photos drive the click. IJM's 4.9 stars do heavy lifting here.
- **Referrals / word of mouth** — a finished backyard a neighbor sees, or a "feels like family" client telling a friend. Huge in home services.
- **Seeing a real project** — a pergola going up down the street, a transformed yard at a friend's barbecue.

**What triggers first awareness:**
1. **Visual** — they see a transformed backyard and think "I want *that*." Often not problem-driven; it's aspiration.
2. **Problem** — an unusable yard plus summer bearing down. The pain finally outweighs the inertia.
3. **Rescue** — they hired someone who ghosted, stalled, or botched it, and they're searching for a contractor who'll actually finish.

## 2. Evaluation

**Questions they ask:**
- "Can they do my *whole* project — pool, pergola, kitchen, concrete — or just one part?"
- "Will they actually show up and finish?" *(The fear underneath everything.)*
- "Can I see what it'll look like before I commit this much money?"
- "What will this really cost, and can I finance it?"
- "Are they licensed and insured? Do they handle the city permits?"
- "How long will it take, and how messy will it be?"

**Comparisons they make:**
- **IJM vs. single-trade pool builders** — IJM wins on full-service + 3D design; the pool-only shop wins on narrow specialization and brand.
- **IJM vs. cheap generalist / handyman** — IJM wins on quality, reliability, warranty, and design vision; the generalist wins on upfront price.
- **IJM vs. DIY / kit** — IJM wins on custom design, permits, and craftsmanship.
- **IJM vs. Angi / Thumbtack** — IJM wins as one accountable builder vs. a bidding free-for-all with five strangers.

**Objections that surface:**
- "It's expensive / it's a luxury." → financing from $149/mo, lasting home value, "you'll use it for years."
- "Contractors never finish." → 4.9 stars across 36 reviews, named crew, rescue stories, licensed since 2007.
- "I can't picture it." → free 3D design of *their own* yard.
- "I'll just hire separate cheaper guys." → one accountable team, coordinated, warrantied — fewer headaches, one number to call.

## 3. Conversion Triggers

**What closes the deal:**
- **The 3D design consultation.** Seeing their own backyard transformed before committing. This is the aha moment reviewers describe by name.
- **Reviews that match their project** — a pool, an outdoor kitchen, a full remodel like the one they want.
- **Financing that makes the monthly doable** — the moment "we can't afford it" becomes "we can swing that."
- **A fast, human first contact.** Dorian or Izaak answering questions and showing up — the opposite of the ghosting they fear. Speed and warmth on the first reply win deals.
- **Free estimate + a clear, calm process** — they know exactly what happens next.

**Proof points that close (ranked):**
1. **4.9 stars, 36 reviews + real local transformations** — social proof from people like them.
2. **Free 3D design** — see it before you buy it.
3. **One team, full-service, permits handled** — the whole backyard, one accountable crew.
4. **Licensed & insured since 2007** — staying power and legitimacy.
5. **Financing from $149/mo** — removes the money objection.

## 4. Stall & Churn Points

**Price / budget stall** — sticker shock on a big number. Reframe with financing and phased scope (start with the pool, add the pergola and kitchen later).

**"Getting other quotes"** — multi-bid limbo. Reviews, 3D design, and obvious reliability are what pull them back.

**Slow follow-up** — the deadliest one. In home services the fast, human responder wins; a lead that sits unseen is a lost job. *(This is why reliable lead routing matters as much as the ad — a form that nobody sees is a customer handed to a competitor.)*

**Ghosting fear left unaddressed** — if the copy and process don't visibly counter the no-show fear, they default to inaction or drift to a "cheaper guy."

**Seasonality** — demand spikes heading into summer and cools in winter. Off-season is for selling *design and planning now, build in spring.*

**Why they don't convert:** went with a cheaper bid (and sometimes come back after a bad experience — the rescue pattern), postponed the project, couldn't get financing approved, or lost to a competitor who simply called back first.

---

## 5. Expansion & Referrals

**Expand the scope within the project:** a pool becomes a pool + pergola + outdoor kitchen + lighting + concrete. IJM's whole edge is doing more of the backyard — every project is a chance to design the *complete* space, not a single feature.

**Phase 2 projects:** they come back next season for the next piece. Stay in touch; the relationship is the asset.

**Referrals are the biggest driver.** A finished backyard others see, plus "feels like family" service, turns clients into advocates. The neighbor at the barbecue is the next lead.

**Reviews are the engine.** Every 5-star review with real project detail feeds Discovery and strengthens the Map Pack moat. Always ask for the review at the moment of delight — the first swim, the first cookout, the final walkthrough.
