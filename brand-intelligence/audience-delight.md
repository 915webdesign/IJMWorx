# Audience Delight Profile

**Company/Brand:** IJM Worx
**Author:** 915 Web Design (David Negrete)

## 1. Who They Are (In Their Own Words)

**How they describe themselves:**
"I want a backyard we actually use." They're El Paso and Borderland homeowners — families, hosts, established homeowners in the Westside, Upper Valley, Northeast, East side, Horizon City, and across the line in Sunland Park. They own their home and they're proud of it. They've got a slab of bare dirt, a tired patio, or an above-ground pool they've outgrown, and they want a real outdoor living space. They describe the dream as a "backyard oasis," a "desert oasis," a "staycation backyard," or "somewhere the family actually wants to hang out." They are buyers, not DIYers — for a project this size they want a pro who designs it, pulls the permits, and builds it start to finish.

**How they want to be seen by their peers:**
The family with the backyard everyone wants to come to. The host. The couple who made a smart, lasting investment in their home instead of another vacation. They want the neighbor to walk in, see the pool and pergola and outdoor kitchen, and say "wait — who built this?"

**Sophistication level: `Beginner-to-Intermediate on construction, Expert on vetting`**
They know what they want — a pool, a pergola, an outdoor kitchen, a fire feature — but not the construction details (gunite vs. fiberglass, paver base depth, permit process). Don't drown them in trade jargon. They ARE expert at one thing: vetting contractors. They read every review, they've been burned or know someone who has, and they can smell a flaky operator. Earn trust fast and show, don't tell.

## 2. What Makes Them Light Up

**Topics they can't stop talking about:**
- **The transformation** — before/after of "ugly dirt to a desert oasis." This is the single most shared, most emotional thing. They want to see real El Paso yards go from nothing to resort.
- **Seeing it first in 3D** — a rendering of *their own* property before anyone breaks ground. It removes the fear of spending big on something they can't picture. Reviewers call this out by name.
- **One team for the whole backyard** — pool *and* pergola *and* kitchen *and* concrete, handled by one crew instead of juggling five contractors.
- **Beating the heat** — shade structures, misting systems, water features, bug lights. Real El Paso-summer problems, solved.
- **Making it affordable** — financing that turns a $40k dream into a monthly number they can actually say yes to.
- **Family and memories** — "a hub for family fun," "countless memories," somewhere to host.

**The "finally someone said it" moments:**
- "I don't want to juggle five different contractors to build one backyard."
- "I want to SEE what it'll look like before I commit to spending this much."
- "Every contractor I called either ghosted me or never showed up."
- "I want it done right — not the cheapest, *right*."
- "We went from ugly dirt to a desert oasis." *(Their words, verbatim, from a real review.)*

**Content they forward to friends and family:**
Real local project photos and videos — actual El Paso backyards, not stock. 3D design walkthroughs. "Here's our process" transparency. Financing explainers that make the leap feel doable. A review from someone whose project looks like theirs.

**What makes them reach out:**
Three trigger moments. (1) **Summer is coming** and they're tired of a backyard nobody uses. (2) **They got burned** — a contractor ghosted them, blew the timeline, or did sloppy work, and they want someone reliable to do it right (or rescue the job). (3) **A life moment** — new house, growing kids, retirement, a raise — and they finally decide to invest in the home.

## 3. What Frustrates Them

**1. Flaky, unreliable contractors**
> "Every contractor I called ghosted me." / "They started the job and disappeared." / "It was supposed to take six weeks and it's been five months."

Why it's unresolved: home-services is full of no-shows, abandoned jobs, and blown timelines. Trust is the scarce resource in this category. This is the #1 emotional barrier — bigger than price.

**2. Can't picture the end result**
> "I know what I kind of want but I can't visualize it." / "How do I spend $50k on something I can't see yet?"

Why it's unresolved: most contractors hand over a vague verbal quote or a flat 2D sketch. The fear of an expensive mistake freezes the decision. (IJM's free 3D design directly dissolves this.)

**3. Juggling multiple trades**
> "The pool guy, the concrete guy, the landscaper, the electrician — nobody talks to each other and everybody points fingers."

Why it's unresolved: single-trade contractors only do their slice, leaving the homeowner as the unpaid general contractor with no single point of accountability.

**Objections and misconceptions they hold:**
- "Outdoor living is a luxury I can't afford right now." *(Financing turns it into a monthly payment — many can afford it, they just don't know it yet.)*
- "Contractors never finish or never show up." *(True of the category — which is exactly why IJM's reviews, named crew, and rescue stories matter so much.)*
- "I'll just hire separate cheaper guys for each part." *(One accountable team that designs and warranties the whole thing usually beats a patchwork — fewer headaches, one number to call.)*

## 4. Their Vocabulary

| Concept | They say | They DON'T say |
|---|---|---|
| The dream | "backyard oasis," "desert oasis," "staycation backyard," "getaway at home" | "exterior environment," "outdoor amenity" |
| The work | "pergola," "shade," "outdoor kitchen," "firepit," "water feature," "pool" | "hardscape elements," "vertical shade structures" |
| Seeing it first | "3D design," "see it before you build it," "rendering of my yard" | "visualization technology," "design deliverables" |
| Trust signals | "they showed up on time," "kept it clean," "finished it right," "feels like family" | "service-level adherence," "project governance" |
| The crew | "the guys," "the crew," "Dorian," "Izaak/Isaac" | "field resources," "the vendor" |
| Local | "the Westside," "Upper Valley," "Horizon," "the Northeast," "EPTX," "915" | "the DMA," "the target geo" |

**In-group / local terms they use without explanation:** desert landscaping, xeriscape, misting system, gunite, plaster, coping, decking, paver patio, retaining wall, sundeck, El Paso heat, monsoon season, HOA approval.

**Terms that signal "vendor speak" (avoid these):** "turnkey outdoor solutions," "best-in-class," "leverage," "comprehensive hardscape services," "elevate your exterior," "synergy," "world-class craftsmanship" (show the craftsmanship instead).

## 5. What Pulls Them In vs. Pushes Them Away

**Pulls them in:**
- Real, local before/after transformations — "this is an actual backyard in your neighborhood."
- "See your yard in 3D before we build it" — de-risks the big spend.
- "One team designs and builds it all" — no juggling contractors.
- Reviews and named people (Dorian, Izaak) — human, accountable, proven.
- "We handle the permits and inspections" — takes the scary part off their plate.
- Financing framed as a monthly number — makes the dream feel reachable.
- A fast, human reply — the business that answers first and warmly often wins.

**Pushes them away:**
- Gatekeeping or aggressive copy — "Serious Homeowners Only," "Not shopping. Not dreaming." This reads as arrogant and cold. *(Note: IJM ads that used this voice tested Poor and clash with the warm, family brand. Lead with welcome, not a velvet rope.)*
- Pressure, scarcity, and fear tactics — "act now or miss out."
- Stock photos that obviously aren't real El Paso projects.
- Vague pricing with no path to affordability.
- Anything that feels like a national lead-gen mill that'll spam them with five random contractors.
- Treating a full backyard transformation like a cheap commodity job.
